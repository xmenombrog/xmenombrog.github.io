Method

From roster to teams

K-Means finds natural player types first; teams are then drafted so each one gets an even share of every type and position.

  • Validate and clean. 62 raw rows: 2 exact duplicates removed, 5 missing values filled with the median or the most common position.
  • Scale and cluster. Six inputs standardized, then K-Means with k chosen by the silhouette peak and the elbow.
  • Compare. Hierarchical clustering (average linkage) agrees on 59 of 60 players, adjusted Rand index 0.958.
  • Draft. Group by group, best player first, each to the team with the fewest of that group, then the fewest players and positions.
  • Refine. Swap players who share a group and position whenever it brings team averages closer together.
  • Tested. 42 pytest cases covering normal, missing, boundary, invalid and uncertain inputs.

Code

Under the hood

One Python module holds the whole process, shared by the batch trainer and the Streamlit interface, with the same steps rebuilt as a KNIME workflow.

SportsDatabaseML/ Python · KNIME
team_balancer.py
Load, validate, clean, scale, cluster, evaluate, name the groups and draft balanced teams.
train.py
Runs the full process and saves the scaler, the model and every output table and chart.
app.py
The Streamlit interface: upload a roster, pick k and the number of teams, place new players.
knime/
The same workflow built in KNIME 5, with a guide to its settings and checked results.
tests/
42 tests across normal, missing, boundary, invalid and uncertain cases, plus team balance.
data/generate_dataset.py
Recreates the synthetic 60-player roster (seed 24), including deliberate data problems.
